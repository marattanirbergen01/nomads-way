## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Felt yurts as low round tents of white felt: a straight drum wall and a smooth shallow domed roof closing at the very top in a small flat wooden ring that lies flush with the felt, the roofline clean and unbroken, the air above every roof clear and still. About 1511, a great gathering on the open steppe on a bright day. In the upper third a vast sky, and below the horizon thousands of horsemen with lances and horsetail standards standing in a huge ring, felt yurts small in the far distance behind them. In the middle of the frame, in the centre of the ring, elders lift a square white felt by its four corners to shoulder height, and on the felt sits Qasym, a tall lean man of about sixty with long hair and a thick beard both heavily streaked with grey, a pointed steel helmet with a gilded band and a thin spike, a grey wolf-fur cloak over a dark green chapan with a gold-embroidered collar, sitting cross-legged with one hand resting on his knee, looking out over the people. In the foreground one of the four corners is held by a young woman with two long black braids in a dark blue quilted chapan, seen from behind. Triumph and unity, the whole steppe looking at one man.
