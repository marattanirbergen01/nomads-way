## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Midday in the square of the town of Sauran, just taken: dust-coloured walls and a burnt gate behind, a light haze of dust in the air. In the middle of the frame a captive prince with a round face and a short black beard, his white turban knocked askew, a dusty faded pale blue robe kneels on the beaten earth with his hands bound before him; behind him a woman in a white headscarf holds two small children close. On the left, mid-sized, stands Burundyq — an older broad-shouldered heavy-set man with a heavy drooping black moustache, a tall domed cap of red and gold brocade on a band of dark fur, a crimson chapan with a gold-embroidered collar over light mail, pointing at the captive, grim. On the right Qasym — a tall lean man of about sixty with long hair and a thick beard both heavily streaked with grey, a pointed steel helmet with a gilded band and a thin spike, a grey wolf-fur cloak over a dark green chapan with a gold-embroidered collar raises his open right hand to stop the execution. A ring of Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and fur hats stands around them, and every one of them has turned to look at Qasym. Tension, a moment frozen.
