## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. The town of Syghnaq: thick walls of rammed earth and sun-dried brick the colour of dust, squat square towers, flat roofs, and one fired-brick mosque with a modest turquoise-tiled dome. A moonless night. Seen from the top of a town tower, looking down over the shoulder of a girl who stands at the parapet in the lower part of the frame: we see only her back, her two long black braids, her small dark red cap with a tuft of white owl feathers, and a bundle of arrows under her arm. Below her, in the middle of the frame, the great wooden gate of the town stands wide open, opened from the inside, and a silent column of horsemen with a few torches rides in through it; at their head rides a young man in a large white turban with a small red cap at its top, a pale blue robe with a row of gold buttons and a yellow sash, his face lifted, cold and still in the torchlight. In the upper third a black sky over the dark flat roofs and towers. Cold blue darkness, warm torchlight only in the gateway. Silence and dread.
