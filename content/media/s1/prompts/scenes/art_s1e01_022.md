## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the 15th-century Kazakh steppe. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — under a wide cool sky, soft volumetric light, wide cinematic composition with a low horizon. Period-accurate nomadic dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed malakhai hats, felt boots, woven belts. Felt yurts as low round tents of white felt: a straight drum wall and a smooth shallow domed roof closing at the very top in a small flat wooden ring that lies flush with the felt, the roofline clean and unbroken, short sturdy steppe horses, Bactrian camels. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. A nomad camp breaking up at sunrise. Yurt felts rolled and lashed onto kneeling Bactrian camels, dismantled lattice walls stacked, dogs and children among the bundles. In the middle ground two sultans stand together on a low rise before a gathered crowd of several dozen people — Kerei and Zhanibek, both in dark chapans and fur-trimmed hats: one speaks with his arm raised, the other stands beside him, calm, a hand resting on his belt; the crowd listens, some already mounted. On the far left a small ancestral burial ground with weathered stone and clay markers. Movement, purpose and quiet grief at once.
