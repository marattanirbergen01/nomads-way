## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Early 1510, winter on the steppe near Syghnaq, thin snow and drifting fog. Seen from a height above the battle, looking down. In the upper third low white fog and a pale sun, the distant dust-coloured walls of Syghnaq faint on the horizon. In the middle third a dense dark mass of heavy cavalry in dark mail and pointed helmets wrapped with a band of white cloth presses forward in the centre, and their dark standard on a tall pole is tilting and falling towards the snow; on both sides of them light Kazakh horsemen in fur hats and quilted coats sweep round in two long curving wings, closing like horns around the heavy riders. In the lower third the snow churned by hooves, a few fallen lances. Motion, the trap closing.
