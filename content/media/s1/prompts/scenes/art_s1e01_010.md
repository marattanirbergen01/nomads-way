## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the 15th-century Kazakh steppe. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate nomadic dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes. All the people are Kazakh nomads with the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches and bare chins. Felt yurts as low round tents of white felt: a straight drum wall and a smooth shallow domed roof closing at the very top in a small flat wooden ring that lies flush with the felt, the roofline clean and unbroken, the air above every roof clear and still. Short sturdy steppe horses, Bactrian camels. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. 1457, the outskirts of Syganak at dawn after an Oirat raid, seen from inside the ruined camp. In the foreground an empty horse pen of broken poles and scattered household goods — an overturned cauldron, torn felt, a dropped saddle. In the middle distance the collapsed lattice walls of two yurts and one yurt still standing with its felt torn open. Far off, tiny on the horizon, a line of Oirat riders leaving in a low cloud of dust. Cold grey-gold morning light, dust hanging in the air. Loss and silence; no people anywhere in the frame.
