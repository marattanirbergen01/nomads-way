A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Dawn in the camp of the Kazakh host on the steppe near the Syr Darya. In the upper third a pale cold dawn sky, and against it the dark shapes of a few low pointed war tents of grey felt and lances planted upright with horsetail standards. In the middle of the frame, beside a small low campfire that burns with a clean bright flame under perfectly clear air, two men, mid-sized, in three-quarter view. On the left, seated on a saddle on the ground, Burundyq — a man of about forty with a very wide, broad, full face, smooth unlined skin, a thin black moustache and a small pointed black goatee, cheeks clean-shaven, black hair; he wears a tall cap of faded dusty-rose brocade that widens towards the top, with large orange-and-gold swirling ornament and a green-and-gold border, and two long brocade flaps hanging down beside his face to his chest; a dark red kaftan with a high standing collar and a dense small woven pattern. His right sleeve is pushed up to the elbow; he holds his bare right forearm out in front of him with a long raw red gash across it, and with his left hand he rips away a strip of blood-soaked linen that was wrapped round it. On the right, crouching, Qasym — a tall lean young man with long black hair to his shoulders and a short black beard, a pointed steel helmet with a gilded band and a thin spike, a grey wolf-fur cloak over a dark green chapan, scratching a plan into the bare dusty earth at his feet with the point of a dagger: a few crude lines, circles and arrows cut into the dirt, the ground around him bare earth and trampled grass. Behind them, small in the distance, a messenger on a lathered horse gallops into the camp, half falling from the saddle. Grey-blue dawn light, the ground of trampled grass and dust.
