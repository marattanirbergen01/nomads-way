## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Dawn in the camp of the Kazakh host on the steppe near the Syr Darya. In the upper third a pale cold dawn sky, and against it the dark shapes of a few low pointed war tents of grey felt and lances planted upright with horsetail standards. In the middle of the frame, beside a small dying campfire, two men, mid-sized, in three-quarter view. On the left, seated on a saddle on the ground, Burundyq — a broad-shouldered heavy-set man with a heavy drooping black moustache, a tall domed cap of red and gold brocade on a band of dark fur, a crimson chapan with a gold-embroidered collar over light mail, tearing a bloodied linen bandage from his right forearm. On the right, crouching, Qasym — a tall lean young man with long black hair to his shoulders and a short black beard, a pointed steel helmet with a gilded band and a thin spike, a grey wolf-fur cloak over a dark green chapan, drawing a map in the dust with the tip of a dagger. Behind them, small in the distance, a messenger on a lathered horse gallops into the camp, half falling from the saddle. Grey-blue dawn light, a thin thread of campfire smoke, the ground of trampled grass and dust.
