## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. The town of Syghnaq: thick walls of rammed earth and sun-dried brick the colour of dust, squat square towers, flat roofs, and one fired-brick mosque with a modest turquoise-tiled dome. This is what the player sees with their own eyes: the player's own figure is NOT in the picture, and the left side of the middle third is kept quiet and dark enough for a portrait to be placed over it. A moonless night outside the eastern wall of Syghnaq, on the open steppe side. In the foreground ten saddled horses stand close together, their reins gathered towards the viewer as if held in the viewer's hands. Beyond them, in the middle of the frame, the great wooden gate in the dark wall is swinging open from the inside, torchlight spilling out of the gateway, and a band of horsemen in dark mail rides out of the darkness past the horses towards the gate. In the upper third the black sky over the long dark line of the wall and its towers. Cold darkness, orange torchlight from the gate. The terrible understanding of whom these horses were for.
