## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Shaybanid court in Mawarannahr. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. 1509, evening inside a rich campaign tent of patterned silk and felt, lit by a brazier and hanging oil lamps; layered carpets on the ground. In the upper third the dark curve of the tent roof with two hanging brass lamps. In the middle of the frame, on the left, an elderly Persian scholar with a long greying beard, a large white turban and a dark brown robe sits cross-legged at a low wooden desk with an open bound notebook, a reed pen raised in his hand. On the right, facing him across the glowing brazier, a slender young prince with a thin black moustache, a neat white turban with a small red cap and a robe of deep green silk sits on cushions and looks down into the fire, thoughtful. Warm firelight, deep shadows, intimate and quiet. The patterned carpets run out of the bottom of the frame.
