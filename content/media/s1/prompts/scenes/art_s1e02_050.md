## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Sunset on top of the town wall of Syghnaq, a thick parapet of rammed earth and sun-dried brick the colour of dust, the day after the battle. In the middle of the frame three people stand side by side at the parapet, seen from behind, looking out over the land: on the left Burundyq, a broad man seen from behind, wearing a tall stiff cap of faded dusty-rose brocade that widens towards the top like a crown, with orange-and-gold swirling ornament and a green-and-gold border and two long brocade flaps hanging down behind his ears, a dark red kaftan with a high standing collar and a dense small woven pattern, his right forearm bound in a linen bandage and resting on the parapet; on the right Qasym, a tall lean young man seen from behind, with long black hair falling to his shoulders, a pointed steel helmet with a gilded band and a thin spike, a grey wolf-fur cloak over a dark green chapan. On the far right, standing at the parapet as a third figure beside Qasym, Dana, a slim girl of sixteen about as tall as Qasym's shoulder, seen from behind in three-quarter view, two long black braids down her back, a small dark red velvet cap with a tuft of white owl feathers, a dark blue sleeveless kamzol over a long white dress, both hands resting on the parapet. Beyond them, filling the upper half, the Syr Darya winds wide and shining through a flat plain of reeds and fields towards the horizon, with a few low flat-roofed mud-brick houses and one turquoise dome in the distance, the low sun large and orange above the water. The dusty top of the wall runs out of the bottom of the frame. Quiet, thoughtful, one peaceful evening on a contested border.
