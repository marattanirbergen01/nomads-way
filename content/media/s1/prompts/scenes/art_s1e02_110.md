## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century deserts of Khorasan. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Autumn 1510, the desert near Merv after the battle. A hot wind lifts a wall of yellow dust that fills the upper half of the sky, the sun a pale disc through it. Out of the dust, in the middle of the frame, walks a single saddled horse with no rider, its reins trailing on the ground. On the sand before its hooves lies a large white turban with a small red cap at its crown, fallen and half covered by sand. The whole picture has no people in it. Emptiness, the end of a man.
