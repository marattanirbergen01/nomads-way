## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the 15th-century Kazakh steppe. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate nomadic dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes. All the people are Kazakh nomads with the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches and bare chins. Felt yurts as low round tents of white felt: a straight drum wall and a smooth shallow domed roof closing at the very top in a small flat wooden ring that lies flush with the felt, the roofline clean and unbroken, the air above every roof clear and still. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Night inside a large yurt, the composition exactly as in the reference picture: the round shanyrak high above with the roof poles radiating from it, felt walls hung with panels of red-and-blue Kazakh oyu ornament, a low fire in the centre throwing warm light up onto two sultans who sit cross-legged facing each other across the hearth, a leather flask and bowls between them, a felt rug beneath. Only the clothing is changed: the sultan on the LEFT is Kerei — a deep blue quilted chapan with a wide collar of gold brocade, and a tall börik with a band of pale fur and a red-and-gold striped crown topped with a white plume, a dark moustache and short goatee, two thin braids beside the cheeks; the sultan on the RIGHT is Zhanibek — a pale gold chapan with a curling ornament, a rounded cap with a cream crown and a deep blue band topped with a white plume, a dark moustache and small pointed goatee. No horses, no animals inside the yurt, no other people. Intimate and tense, warm firelight against the cool night in the roof opening.
