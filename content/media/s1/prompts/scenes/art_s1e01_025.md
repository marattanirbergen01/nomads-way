## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the 15th-century Kazakh steppe. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — under a wide cool sky, soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate nomadic dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed malakhai hats, felt boots, woven belts. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. A nomad caravan on the winter march, seen from directly behind: the whole column moves away from the viewer into the depth of the frame, toward a low horizon, every rider, camel and horse shown from the back — no face turned toward the viewer anywhere. Camels loaded with rolled felts and lattice walls, riders hunched in quilted chapans and fur hats, a few on foot leading horses, breath steaming from people and animals in the cold. Low winter sun in haze ahead of them, long blue shadows on the snow, dry grass poking through the crust. Open steppe with nothing built on it; the column grows smaller and smaller until it fades into the haze at the horizon. Weariness and stubbornness, no faces.
