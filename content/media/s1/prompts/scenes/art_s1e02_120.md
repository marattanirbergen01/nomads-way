## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. About 1511, dawn on a steppe road running south. In the upper third a pale sky over a line of low hills, the road disappearing towards them. In the middle of the frame a small caravan — a few laden camels, a covered cart, a dozen riders — sets off down the road; at its head a tired man of about fifty-five on horseback, seen in three-quarter view from behind, a tall cap of faded dusty-rose brocade with orange-and-gold swirling ornament and two long brocade flaps hanging down behind his ears, a dark red kaftan with a high standing collar and a dense small woven pattern, his shoulders bowed, grey hair at the nape. On the right, at the roadside, Qasym — a tall lean man of about sixty with long hair and a thick beard both heavily streaked with grey, a pointed steel helmet with a gilded band and a thin spike, a grey wolf-fur cloak over a dark green chapan with a gold-embroidered collar sits on horseback at the head of a guard of honour of twenty riders with lances and long horsetail standards, all of them with heads bowed in farewell. Road dust and dry grass run out of the bottom of the frame. Quiet, dignified, melancholy.
