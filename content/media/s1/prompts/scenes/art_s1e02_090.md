## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the late 15th-century Kazakh steppe and the towns of the Syr Darya. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes; Kazakh warriors in quilted coats and light mail, pointed steel helmets or fur hats, bows and lances. All the steppe people have the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches. Short sturdy steppe horses. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. Felt yurts as low round tents of white felt: a straight drum wall and a smooth shallow domed roof closing at the very top in a small flat wooden ring that lies flush with the felt, the roofline clean and unbroken, the air above every roof clear and still. 1509, dawn in the low rocky mountains of Ulytau, pale rose light on bare stony hills in the upper third. In the middle of the frame a burned camp: dark rings of cold ash where yurts once stood, charred lattice walls lying on the ground, overturned pots; the fires are long dead and the air is clear. In the centre, seen in three-quarter view from behind, Qasym — a tall lean man of about sixty with long hair and a thick beard both heavily streaked with grey, a pointed steel helmet with a gilded band and a thin spike, a grey wolf-fur cloak over a dark green chapan with a gold-embroidered collar lifts a blackened round wooden roof ring of a yurt in both hands, holding it up against the sky. From the left, small in the middle distance, a woman with two long black braids in a dark blue quilted chapan, a bow and a quiver across her back, her face not visible runs towards him across the ash. Grim, but with the hope of dawn.
