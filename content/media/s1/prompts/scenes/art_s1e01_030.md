## Prompt
A painterly semi-realistic historical illustration of the 15th-century Kazakh steppe. Muted earth palette — ochre, dust, faded indigo, weathered leather brown — soft volumetric light, a tall vertical composition for a phone screen: sky in the upper third, the action in the middle third, the ground running out of the bottom of the frame. Period-accurate nomadic dress: quilted chapan robes, fur-trimmed hats, felt boots, woven sashes. All the people are Kazakh nomads with the faces of the steppe: broad flat cheekbones, narrow dark eyes, thin moustaches and bare chins. Felt yurts as low round tents of white felt: a straight drum wall and a smooth shallow domed roof closing at the very top in a small flat wooden ring that lies flush with the felt, the roofline clean and unbroken, the air above every roof clear and still. Short sturdy steppe horses, Bactrian camels. No plate armour, no fantasy elements, no modern objects, no lettering anywhere in the image. 1465, the Chu river valley in spring, seen from a low rise. In the upper third snow-capped mountains and a vast sky with soft clouds. In the middle third the wide bright river winding through green grassland, and on the near bank — on dry grass, well back from the water, never in the river — a caravan that has only just arrived: no yurt is standing yet; the bare wooden lattice walls of two yurts are being raised in circles with no felt on them, rolled felts and lattice sections lie stacked beside kneeling camels being unloaded, horses are turned out to graze. In the foreground a few people stand with their backs to the viewer looking out over the valley and the mountains, others work at the camp, all small in the frame, no faces toward the viewer. Golden afternoon light. Arrival and a new beginning.
